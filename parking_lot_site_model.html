Bespoke Underwriting Parking Lot Site Evaluation Model

Site evaluation for a surface parking lot on College Park Rd Lots 1 & 2 in Conway, SC, near Coastal Carolina University, where Santee Cooper transmission easements cover most of the land. It covers site yield, total project cost, revenue by season, operating expenses, NOI, payback, IRR and the land price the deal can support, in a low case (50 spaces at $5 / day) and a high case (80 spaces at $10 / day). Every output recalculates as you edit an input.

Alternative: medical or office pad

Modeled in Step Five
  • Zoning. Conway's P district permits offices and medical / dental offices by right. Stand-alone parking needs a special exception (UDO §5.1.33). New buildings may cover at most 30% of the lot.
  • Layout. A building would sit on the roughly 10,665 sf pad outside the easements, with its parking in the easement, subject to Santee Cooper's written consent. A realistic footprint is about 4,000-6,000 sf (estimate), depending on whether stormwater goes in an underground vault or a surface basin. Two stories is the cleanest option.
  • Asking rents in Conway. General office: $12-$16 / sf / yr NNN. Medical office: $21.60-$32.00 / sf / yr. The $21.60 ask is a fixed monthly rent, and the $32.00 ask is a new turnkey clinic next to Conway Medical Center whose lease type is not disclosed.
  • Bottom line at default inputs. At the $225,000 ask, the parking lot is a no-go on the full pro forma. The high case clears a 10% yield on cost but takes 6.6 years to pay back. The best use of the site is likely a medical or office pad outside the easement with parking in the easement.

Notes on the office and shared parking

ESTIMATE
  • Parking minimum. Shared parking still has to satisfy the office minimum of 1 space per 300 sf (about 36 spaces at the full buildable sf, computed live). The lot has to supply them, likely through a shared-parking agreement or city approval, and they may overlap paid daytime parking. Reserved spaces are taken out of paid parking all year here, which is conservative for evenings, weekends and game days.
  • Footprint. Conway's P district caps a new building's footprint at 30% of the lot (about 17.7K sf here), which is a separate limit. The memos put a realistic one-story footprint at about 4,000-6,000 sf with a surface stormwater basin, or about 6,000 sf with an underground vault, so 10,665 sf is an upper bound. Two stories is the cleanest fit. The stormwater pond or vault area still applies and can't go in the easement.
  • Premium event layer. Premium revenue = spaces × occupancy × premium rate × days for each venue, added to normal seasonal revenue, which is unchanged. Premium days may coincide with normal busy days; per Michael, the premium is treated as incremental income. Only Brooks Stadium football has defaults: 6 home games in 2025 and 2026; low rate $20.83 = the $125 GG / YY 2026 season pass ÷ 6 games; high rate $25 = GG lot per car (Road To CFB, Nov 2024); occupancy 75% is an assumption. Other venues are pending research, with days set to 0.
  • Build cost (estimate). RSMeans 1-story office $235-$375 / sf (2025, national); BuildGenius 2026 South Carolina office $198 / sf; Mastt 2026 low-rise office $240-$440 / sf, soft costs typically 10-15% of hard costs. No Conway bid was found.

How it works. Year-1 figures drive the key metrics. Usable area = gross area − pole clearance (poles × π × 50 ft²) − stormwater pond − setbacks; spaces = usable area ÷ sf per space, capped at the plan. Revenue = spaces × daily rate × busy days × occupancy by season (academic term, summer / break, events), less collection loss = effective gross revenue (EGR). NOI = EGR − property tax, insurance, maintenance, lighting power, payment app / enforcement, management and reserves. Yield on cost = NOI ÷ total project cost; simple payback = total project cost ÷ NOI. The 5-year hold grows EGR and opex each year and sells at the end of Year 5 on Year-6 NOI ÷ exit cap, less sale costs. Max land price solves for the price that meets the target payback after closing costs, hard costs and soft costs.

What is not included. Income taxes and depreciation (all returns are pre-tax); office pad site work, utilities and building stormwater (Step Five covers building cost, rent and value); and an operator lease structure (fixed rent or a revenue share), since the model is owner-operated as shown.
